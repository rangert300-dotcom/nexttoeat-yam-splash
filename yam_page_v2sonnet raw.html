The Origin

Why Y.A.M. Exists

G od put it in Gary Netter's heart to create Y.A.M. — because he's trying to be the person he was looking for when he was younger.

This isn't a grant-funded initiative looking for a mission statement. This is a man who traveled the world, cooked for important people, built something — and then heard a calling: come back. Come back to Central City. Come back to your people. Come back and build what wasn't there when you needed it.

Gary already fought the food desert in the neighborhood he grew up in. Now he's moving the Wholistic Culinary Market to Oretha Castle Haley Boulevard — a street where Black people were once allowed to shop but not allowed to work. He's not just opening a building. He's closing a loop that's been open for generations.

"Real progress starts when we stop waiting to be included and start investing in ourselves."

The Identity

You Are a Yammer

At Y.A.M., you're not a participant. You're not a beneficiary. You are a Yammer — and Yammers lead departments, train each other, and move through cohorts coming out the other side with skills, community, and a sense of self that nobody can take.

"I Yam What I Yam" is the whole philosophy. Show up as yourself. Grow from there. No apologies, no performance — just presence.

Yammers don't wait to be included. Yammers build the table.

The Mission

Purpose & Impact

New Orleans is famous for its culture, music, artistry, and celebrations. The world comes here to experience creativity and history. Yet many young people in this city don't have consistent, safe spaces to express themselves, protect their mental health, learn healthy habits, and feel supported — especially during after-school hours.

Too many youth are carrying stress and trauma while having limited access to care, stable opportunities, and creative outlets. Y.A.M. exists to change that narrative — and to provide a sanctuary where young adults can heal, grow, and flourish.

Safe Space

Create a safe, affirming space for young adults — full stop.

Belonging

Reduce isolation while increasing belongingness and authentic self-expression.

Alternatives

Offer positive alternatives to harmful systems by investing in joy and creativity.

Food Justice

Bridge art, advocacy and wellness — with food consciousness as the foundation.

The Path

Curriculum & Leadership

Our multi-year program offers diverse learning routes: Culinary Medicine, Business, Food Science, Agriculture, Music, Visual Arts, and more. Youth join as a cohort and take on real leadership roles — overseeing departments, training peers, and building skills over two to three years.

Participants work alongside pop-up vendors, botanists, and practitioners, with a clear commitment to Saturday programming. We connect young adults to stakeholders who support their growth — bridging learning directly to workforce readiness and beyond.
